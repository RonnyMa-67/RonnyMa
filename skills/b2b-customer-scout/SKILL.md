# B2B Customer Scout

[更新记录 / Changelog](../CHANGELOG.md#b2b-customer-scout)

Research potential business customers and return a practical, evidence-backed outreach list. Adapt to the user's industry, country, product and preferred language. Ask targeted questions only when an unanswered detail materially changes which companies qualify; continue independent research while waiting.

## 1. Define the target

- Read available product catalogues, previous customer lists and feedback. Identify how the product works, compatible inputs, application, buyer types, purchase channels, OEM options and exclusions. Distinguish lookalike products with different operating principles.
- Record requested geography, company count, products, buyer profiles and exclusions. Deduplicate prior results using trading names, domains, legal entities and group aliases; retain separate group companies only when the user allows it and label the relationship.
- Search for manufacturers, importers, distributors, ingredient suppliers, equipment operators, brands using similar products, and relevant competitors or their channel partners as appropriate. Sales channel alone is weaker evidence than a relevant existing product or demonstrated use.

## 2. Search using product images and specifications

When an image or catalogue is available, inspect it and identify distinguishing visual and technical features. Search images, model numbers, alternate names, local languages, competitor brands, websites, social media, marketplaces, trade fairs and maps. Follow images to the actual company and product page.

Check what is visible in a company image against its specifications and description. A similar exterior or a generic scene is only a lead, not proof of the same mechanism. Distinguish manufacturer, importer, marketplace seller and distributor; avoid assuming an image proves current stock or purchase intent. State whether the match is same model, comparable operation, or only a possible channel.

## 3. Research company and people

For each prospect, identify its business, products, sales channels, customer base and location from public sources. Explain in concrete terms why its existing business matches the user's offer. Look for product pages, catalogues, trade-fair listings, company posts and recent local evidence. Follow leads into company websites and social platforms; do not claim every platform was checked when it was inaccessible.

Find named owners, purchasing managers or relevant senior commercial leaders. Investigate their current role and seek direct published business email, telephone, WhatsApp or a personal professional profile. Preserve a named decision-maker even if only a company contact can be found, and make the gap visible. Attribute every number and email to the correct person or company; never invent, infer or unmask contact details. Label dated directories, uncertain employment, regional head-office contacts and unverified purchasing authority. Public WhatsApp links establish a business contact route, not its current availability or ownership by a named individual.

Separate observed facts from sales hypotheses. Do not claim that an ingredient trader buys a particular machine, that a product listing is an import, that a person has purchasing authority, or that a buyer has expressed interest without evidence. If the strongest evidence is for an adjacent segment, mark the lead as channel expansion rather than a confirmed product match. If there are not enough supported leads, report the actual count and gaps; do not pad the list.

## 4. Deliver and plan visits

Use the user's requested format; otherwise prefer a concise spreadsheet for a multi-company outreach list. For each row include company and website, product category, background, reason for fit, named contact and role, contact routes with ownership, location and map link, and a blank follow-up field. Include product, personnel and contact source links plus dates and material uncertainty in a supporting table. Provide reference image links or a visual comparison when image matching is central.

When visits are requested, identify the address type and source: head office, factory, showroom, warehouse or directory listing. Link to the exact verified map place where available; label company/address searches and approximate locations as searches, not verified pins. Group geographically plausible appointments and provide map directions; identify unconfirmed premises and do not invent travel times, opening hours, appointments or route optimization. Prioritize online contact when the user requests it.

## 5. Incorporate feedback

Record which companies the user or sales team accepted, rejected or contacted and why. Update the targeting criteria and deduplication list before the next batch. Preserve earlier deliverables and label revisions. Provide the requested customer count per category where supported, without treating repeated brands or subsidiaries as independent companies automatically.

## Publication and tools

Use browsing and current primary sources when available. Crawl4AI, map scrapers, search and social research can assist if available and appropriate; do not imply they have run when they have not. Respect access restrictions and use only publicly accessible business information. Do not contact prospects or publish customer lists unless the user authorizes that action. Do not publish private customer lists, catalogues, personal details or credentials as examples without permission.
